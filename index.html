Left-wing Federal Supreme Court judges are much more likely to uphold appeal

SWITZERLAND According to our analysis, the Swiss Federal Supreme Court is significantly more likely to uphold criminal appeals when a majority of the three-judge panel belongs to a left-wing political party.

By Mischa Aebi

Judges are expected to decide cases independently. Yet they are repeatedly accused of being influenced by their political affiliation. One example came five years ago, when the Swiss Federal Supreme Court ruled that landlords could earn substantially higher returns on rental properties. Not a single left-wing judge sat on the five-member panel. More controversially, the judges justified their decision by referring to demands that had also been made by centre-right politicians in Parliament.

Our analysis of several thousand criminal rulings now provides the first statistical evidence that left-leaning and right-leaning Federal Supreme Court judges decide cases differently. To conduct the analysis, we examined the Court's 10,000 most recent judgments. Because most cases are decided by three-judge panels, we limited our dataset to those rulings.

Right-leaning three-judge panels make disproportionately many rulings

We then analysed all cases in which two of the three judges belonged either to right-of-centre parties or to left-wing parties. Cases in which judges from centrist parties formed the majority were excluded from the analysis.
